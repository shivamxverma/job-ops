# Voice — how Shivam writes

Derived from his own outreach system (`founder-outreach-ai/outreach/`: the DM framework, his
good examples and the anti-patterns he wrote down himself). **Read `writing-samples.md` before
drafting anything.** Generated outreach and form answers must sound like this, then get one
human pass. If output could have been written by anyone, it fails.

## Traits
- Problem-first. He opens with *their* product and *their* likely bottleneck, not with himself.
- Solutions, not stacks. He names what he built and the mechanism that solved it ("persistent
  Docker containers to keep worker throughput high"), never a bare list of technologies.
- Specific and bounded. One project, one problem, one mechanism per message. He does not claim
  he can "build your entire backend".
- Low-pressure close. "Open to a quick chat?" He doesn't beg, and he doesn't call himself "eager
  to learn": he shows up as someone who speeds a team up, not someone they have to carry.
- Short. A DM is four beats and signs off "- Shivam".

## The 4-beat structure (cold DM / founder email)
1. **Hook:** "I noticed you're building [product/feature]." Proof he looked.
2. **Bottleneck:** "One challenge you'll likely face is [specific engineering problem]."
3. **Proof:** "I recently built [project] where I solved a similar problem using [mechanism]."
4. **Ask:** "I think I could help accelerate [that area]. Open to a quick chat?"

## Anti-patterns (his own list)
- **The tech dumper:** "I am a passionate Full Stack Developer. I know React, Node.js, ...".
  That makes the founder work out where the skills fit.
- **The generic beggar:** "I love your mission... I'm eager to learn and contribute... I built
  a chat app and a to-do list." Zero specific value.
- **The hallucinator:** "I am an expert in Python. I can build your entire backend." Too broad
  to believe.

## Rules for generated text
1. Every claim carries a number, a name, or a link, and traces to `facts.yaml`. If it can't, cut
   it.
2. Lead with the checkable artifact, not the adjective.
3. Never write "leverage", "passionate", "thrilled", "seamless", "align perfectly", "proven
   track record" or "eager to learn".
4. Mirror at most ONE specific phrase from the target company's own writing, never generic
   industry buzzwords.
5. Keep cold outreach 50–125 words. No resume attached on first contact unless asked; a repo or
   live link instead.
6. **Never use an em dash (—) in anything Shivam sends** (emails, DMs, HN replies, cover
   letters, extension autofill answers). It is the most recognised "an AI wrote this" tell.
   Split into two sentences, or use a comma, colon, or parentheses. En dashes in date ranges
   ("Nov 2025 – Mar 2026") are fine.
7. Contractions, plain words, varied sentence length. If a line reads balanced, hedged and
   evenly cadenced, it fails.
8. He is employed at poiro.com. Outreach reads as a deliberate search for the next hard problem,
   never as escaping a bad job, and never mentions poiro internals beyond what `facts.yaml`
   states.
